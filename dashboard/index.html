What you should and shouldn't take from this

  • Personal, not population. Every score compares you to your own pre-flight baseline. We deliberately avoid implying a population "normal." n=4 is too small to define one.
  • "Within baseline noise" means we can't tell. If a post-flight value sits inside the spread of your three pre-flight swabs, we say so explicitly. That's not "you recovered." It's "we can't distinguish from your normal variation."
  • Color = "shifted," not "dangerous." The avatar's red regions show how far a body site's microbial community has moved from your own pre-flight baseline. It doesn't distinguish a beneficial shift from a harmful one. To get direction, open the drilldown: ⚠ marks taxa whose shift may matter (a literature-flagged opportunist rising, or a beneficial commensal falling); ✓ marks shifts that may be favorable (a beneficial commensal rising, or an opportunist falling). Curated lists with citations on hover.
  • No clinical recommendations. This is a research-data viewer, not a medical tool. Anything actionable should go through your flight surgeon.
  • No in-flight CBC. Blood draws happened before and after the mission, not during. The CBC plots intentionally have a gap between L-3 and R+1, and we don't interpolate across it.
